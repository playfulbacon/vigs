# Vancouver Indie Games: Community Infrastructure and Draft-Timeline Gaps

Research date: 2026-11-07. About 20 tool calls. Several primary pages (digibc.org/about, PressReader Georgia Straight print edition) returned 403, and fullindie.com/history returned 404. Facts from those pages appear only where another page or a search snippet confirmed them, and are marked that way.

## 1. The indie scene before 2008

### Takeaway
Almost no published evidence turned up for a distinct Vancouver indie scene before 2008. The few traces are the Northways starting out at the University of Victoria in the 2000s, the Vancouver International Game Summit (the conference that came before GDC Canada), and New Media BC (founded 1997). This is the biggest gap for interviews.

### Cited Findings
- The Northways "started making indie games in the 2000s, while at the University of Victoria"; Sarah Northway calls 2008 "the feted year of Flash." — [Georgia Straight, V. S. Wells, 27 Mar 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- GDC Canada 2009 drew 800+ attendees, which coverage described as double the previous year's Vancouver International Game Summit. So a Vancouver industry conference existed before 2009 (exact years not confirmed). — [Game Developer: GDC Canada 2009 round-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-canada-2009-the-event-round-up); [UBM press release](https://ubmchannelnews.mediaroom.com/index.php?item=87578)
- DigiBC's own materials say it was founded in 1997 as New Media BC and merged with WinBC (wireless) in 2009. A later name was "DigiBC – The Digital Media + Wireless Association of BC"; it now calls itself "the Creative Technology Association of BC." — [DigiBC 2017 Year in Review](https://digibc.org/?p=8144) (search snippet; the about page returned 403); [Game Rebellion directory](https://db.gamerebellion.com/associations/digibc-the-creative-technology-association-of-bc)

### Inferences
- Before 2008, independent work around Vancouver seems to have been individual Flash and shareware developers (the Northways in Victoria), not an organised scene. The first organised indie-only body appears to be the May 2010 meetup.

### Gaps
- No sources found on: the Vancouver demoscene, mod communities, shareware or Flash developers in Vancouver before 2008, or early indie studios other than Klei (2005).
- IGDA Vancouver founding date and early leaders: not found. The current chapter page lists Kevin Brunt and Christopher James as leaders ([IGDA BC–Vancouver](https://igda.org/?p=6710)), and it held meetings in 2025 and 2026 ([IGDA news, June 17 2025 meeting](https://igda.org/news-archive/igda-vancouver-june-17th-2025-meeting/)). An undated DigiBC post mentions an ongoing "attempt at reviving IGDA Vancouver" ([DigiBC tag page](https://www.digibc.org/tags/video-games-1)), so the chapter has been dormant and revived at least once. Interview question: who ran IGDA Vancouver in the 2000s, and when did it lapse?
- Vancouver International Game Summit: which years it ran and who organised it (likely Reboot Communications, which co-produced GDC Canada; not confirmed).

## 2. The Victoria meetup that seeded Full Indie

### Takeaway
The only published source is Sarah Northway's quote in the Georgia Straight. A roommate from Victoria started an indie meetup there and "brought it with him" to Vancouver. The roommate is not named. Jake Birkett's own account of the first Vancouver meetup doesn't mention Victoria.

### Cited Findings
- "Our roommate from Victoria, he started a meet-up in Victoria for indie devs, and when he moved to Vancouver, I think he brought it with him." Sarah also describes "just this really small community in 2010." — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- Birkett's blog post of 22 May 2010: he and "Alex Vostrov, another Vancouver resident" set up the Vancouver Indie Game Developer Meetup. The first meetup was at Soho Cafe and Billiards in Yaletown, with 40+ attendees out of 60+ members. No earlier meetups or Victoria are mentioned. — [Grey Alien Games blog](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/first-vancouver-indie-game-developer-meetup-was-a-big-success/)
- A TIGSource forum thread advertised the Vancouver meetup (not opened). — [TIGSource](https://forums.tigsource.com/index.php?topic=12545.0)
- An IGDA Victoria chapter exists today (itch.io page). Its founding date is unknown. — [igdavictoria.itch.io](https://igdavictoria.itch.io)

### Inferences
- The roommate is plausibly Alex Vostrov, since he is the co-founder other than Birkett (who came from the UK). This is not confirmed by any source; Birkett simply calls Vostrov "another Vancouver resident."

### Gaps
- Who the roommate was, when the Victoria meetup ran, and where. Ask Sarah and Colin Northway and Alex Vostrov directly.
- Whether Jake Birkett ever lived in Victoria: no evidence found. Birkett left Vancouver in 2012 ([Full Indie About](https://www.fullindie.com/about)).

## 3. Full Indie: full history

### Takeaway
Full Indie is a Vancouver nonprofit with these milestones:
- **2010:** founded by Birkett and Vostrov.
- **2011:** rebranded as Full Indie.
- **2013:** first Summit.
- **2016:** presidency passes from Vostrov to Maurice Grela.
- **About 2020–2023:** hiatus during Covid.
- **2023:** Aryo Nazaradeh restarts meetups.
- **2024:** Summit returns at Emily Carr.
- **2025:** Summit at the Vancouver Playhouse.
- **2026:** Summit at the Westin Bayshore.

Chloe Ezra is now listed as president.

### Cited Findings
- **Founding and identity**
  - Full Indie calls itself "a Vancouver-based nonprofit." The first meetup was at a Yaletown bar, and attendance exceeded the venue's capacity. The group was first called the Vancouver Indie Game Developers.
  - Its logo and branding came from Su Skerl and Jason Elliot, and it was renamed "Full Indie" months later.
  - Birkett left Vancouver in 2012.
  - — [Full Indie About](https://www.fullindie.com/about)
  - After nine meetings, Jake and Alex decided a formal brand and web presence were needed; FullIndie.com launched in 2011. Membership was 2,000+ as of 2014. A UK branch, FullIndie.uk (formerly Best of British), started in 2012. — [Wikipedia: Full Indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie)
  - At its peak Full Indie "counted 5,000 members." — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- **Presidents**
  - Vostrov (2010–2016), then Maurice Grela (2016 to about 2023), then Aryo Nazaradeh, who resumed meetups in 2023 "after a five-year hiatus that began during the COVID-19 pandemic." The current team page lists Chloe Ezra (President), Miranda (VP), Amelia Wales (Treasurer), Vivan Huang (Secretary) and Demi Montes (Community Manager). The 2024 team also included Nikita Kotter. — [Full Indie About](https://www.fullindie.com/about)
  - Conflict on dates: the Georgia Straight says Nazaradeh "took the helm after returning to the city from Toronto in 2022." Full Indie's site says meetups resumed in 2023. The draft timeline says 2022.
  - The "five-year hiatus" wording also doesn't fit cleanly with a 2020 start and a 2023 restart. Ask about this.
  - Nazaradeh said Full Indie "just never started back up because the people running it didn't have time anymore." When he began organising meetups, Full Indie offered him its email list and then the role. — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- **Programs and partnerships**
  - 2017: Game Curious, with Hand Eye Society at the Richmond Public Library (drop-in arcades and a six-week game-making program).
  - 2018: the Comics <3 Games showcase with VanCAF (Vancouver Comic Arts Festival).
  - Game jams were held at the Centre for Digital Media, and demo nights at Vancouver Film School.
  - — [Full Indie About](https://www.fullindie.com/about)
- **Meetup venues after the restart**
  - The Gaming Stadium in Richmond, with 100+ attending monthly (as of 2024). — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
  - Microsoft Vancouver and LaSalle College in 2025, per the draft's sources.
- **Summit by year**
  - **2013:** The first summit was a response to studios and GDC Canada leaving. It was held at the Rio Theatre ([Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/?p=7577)). Speakers included Mass Effect and Mark of the Ninja developers, and coverage gave an April 10 date ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie), [Engadget, 10 Apr 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-10-full-indie-summit-brings-vancouver-dev-industry-together.html)). Vancouver Weekly reportedly gives April 20. The date is unresolved.
  - **2015:** Vancouver Convention Centre, according to a search snippet from an East Side Games Medium post ([Medium](https://medium.com/east-side-games/east-side-indie-love-from-a-summit-filled-with-indies-5bbbda79e528), not opened). Talks went online ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/videos-full-indie-summit-2015)).
  - **2016:** Speakers included Nels Anderson (Firewatch) ([Meetup event](https://www.meetup.com/Vancouver-Indie-Game-Developers/events/234106569), snippet).
  - **2017:** Expanded to a full weekend. Speakers included Nick Suttner (Sony), Dan Adelman (ex-Nintendo), and Chet Faliszek and Aaron Leiby (Valve) ([Daily Hive, Dec 2017](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/full-indie-summit), snippet). Daily Hive calls this the "second year," probably meaning the second year in the expanded format; this conflicts with the 2013 start.
  - **2019:** "650+ creators each year" ([DigiBC event listing](https://www.digibc.org:443/events/2019-full-indie-summit), snippet).
  - **2024:** "The long awaited Full Indie Summit will return," on Sat 21 Sep 2024 at Emily Carr University, 520 E 1st Ave ([DigiBC, 28 Jun 2024](https://digibc.org/2024/06/28/video-game-the-full-indie-summit-is-on-for-2024); [Events for Gamers](https://www.eventsforgamers.com/event/full-indie-summit-2024)).
  - **2025:** Vancouver Playhouse, 13 Sep 2025, ten speakers ([Vancouver Civic Theatres](https://vancouvercivictheatres.com/events/full-indie-summit-2025-sep-13/)).
  - **2026:** Westin Bayshore, 20 Sep 2026, 20+ speakers ([Luma](https://luma.com/avvyh3gy)). PocketGamer lists it as a two-day event ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/events/full-indie-summit-2026/)), which conflicts with Luma.

### Inferences
- The last pre-Covid Summit was probably 2019. No 2020–2023 summits were found, which fits the hiatus.

### Gaps
- Summit venues for 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019, and full speaker lists.
- Full Indie Game Jam years beyond 2011 and 2012.
- "Full Indie UP" and the Full Indie Holiday Party: no sources found.
- When Full Indie formally registered as a nonprofit (BC Societies Act), and when Nazaradeh handed over to Chloe Ezra.

## 4. Other organizations, events and schools

### Takeaway
GDC Canada ran in Vancouver only in 2009 and 2010. DigiBC (founded 1997 as New Media BC) is the industry body and lobbied for the tax credit. Newer grassroots events include Vancouver Game Garden (from 2024, co-organised by Greg Lobanov). Most other named items had no evidence found.

### Cited Findings
- **GDC Canada**
  - 2009: Vancouver Convention Centre, 800+ attendees, produced by Think Services and Reboot Communications. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-canada-2009-the-event-round-up)
  - 2010: returned to the Vancouver Convention Centre on May 6–7, 2010, and offered free passes to laid-off developers. — [Newswire](https://newswire.ca/news-releases/canadian-game-development-community-to-reconvene-at-the-second-annual-gamedevelopers-conferencetm-canada-may-6-7-2010-539206541.html); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/2010-gdc-canada-offers-free-passes-for-laid-off-developers)
  - Engadget (2013) wrongly says the "first, and last" GDC Canada was in 2010. No official cancellation notice was found. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-10-full-indie-summit-brings-vancouver-dev-industry-together.html)
- **DigiBC:** credited with getting the IDMTC introduced in 2010. Brenda Bailey was executive director at one point; she later became a BC MLA and minister (that last part is my own background knowledge, not checked). — DigiBC search snippets ([digibc.org](https://digibc.org/))
- **Vancouver Game Garden:** an annual two-day showcase of 40+ Vancouver-made games, with an artist alley and talks. The 2025 edition (its second year) ran 27–28 Sep 2025 at the Roundhouse Community Centre. Lobanov is co-organiser. It was inspired by VanCAF in May 2024. — [Georgia Straight, 24 Sep 2025](https://www.straight.com/arts/you-nearly-missed-local-indie-games-bloom-at-vancouver-game-garden)
- **VanCAF crossover:** Full Indie and VanCAF hosted Comics <3 Games in 2018. — [Full Indie About](https://www.fullindie.com/about)
- **Global Game Jam:** two popular Vancouver Global Game Jams came before Full Indie's own jam (2011). This is from the draft's existing CGM citation; no new detail found.

### Gaps
- No evidence gathered on these:
  - Vancouver Game Jam
  - Game Dev Vancouver
  - Pixelles-like groups and Ladies Learning Code game events
  - Vancouver Indie Game Showcases
  - Indie Game Night
  - Play It Forward
  - Vancouver Gaming Expo
  - Canadian Game Awards
  - PAX in Vancouver (none known)
- Founding dates for the school programs (VFS Game Design, Centre for Digital Media, SFU SIAT, Emily Carr, Capilano, LaSalle) were not researched for lack of budget.

## 5. Government and policy

### Takeaway
BC's Interactive Digital Media Tax Credit was announced in February 2010 at 17.5% of BC labour and took effect 1 Sep 2010. It was renewed several times (to 2018, then to 2023). Budget 2025 proposed raising it to 25% and making it permanent from 1 Sep 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **2010 introduction**
  - Proposed in February 2010 as a 17.5% labour credit. At first, "no information provided as to how [it] will work." — [Georgia Straight 2010](https://www.straight.com/blogra/bc-video-game-industry-gets-some-support-new-175-percent-labour-tax-credit)
  - Applies to projects beginning after 31 Aug 2010. — [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/film-media-tax-credits/british-columbia-refundable-media-tax-credits.html)
  - The BC Interactive Task Force welcomed it. — [Newswire](https://newswire.ca/news-releases/bc-interactive-task-force-welcomes-proposed-bc-interactive-digital-media-taxcredit-for-video-game-development-539264911.html)
- **Renewals**
  - Originally set to expire in 2015; renewed to 2018. — [Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/?p=97044)
  - Developers lobbied for renewal. — [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/life/584451/video-game-developers-want-bc-renew-interactive-digital-media-tax-credit)
  - CRA Schedule 429 covers wages "before September 1, 2023." — [CRA T2SCH429 (via CCH)](https://www.cchwebsites.com/content/pdf/tax_forms/ca/en/t2sch429.pdf)
- **2025 change:** Budget 2025 proposed raising the rate to 25% for wages incurred after 31 Aug 2025 and making the credit permanent. — [BC Gov IDMTC page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/corporate/credits/interactive-digital-media); [Budget 2025 Backgrounder 3](https://bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2025/accessibility/2025_Backgrounder_3.htm); [BIV](https://www.biv.com/news/budget-brings-optimism-to-bc-video-game-industry-10343112), which says it could help the smallest studios.
- **Competition from other provinces**
  - A 2012 Gamasutra piece flagged a talent drain to Montreal and Toronto because of their tax incentives. — [Game Developer: What's really going down in Vancouver](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/what-s-really-going-down-in-vancouver-)
  - Maclean's covered developers moving to Ontario for its credits. — [Maclean's](https://macleans.ca/economy/business/better-tax-credits-lure-b-c-video-game-makers-to-ontario)
  - Calgary was courting Vancouver developers in 2018. — [BIV 2018](https://biv.com/article/2018/09/why-alberta-poses-new-threat-bc-gaming-industry)

### Inferences
- The IDMTC is a labour-based credit, so it mainly helps incorporated studios with payroll. Its effect on solo or house-share indies was probably indirect. Ask interviewees whether they ever claimed it.

### Gaps
- How the 2018–2023 extension came about: confirmed only indirectly by the CRA form.
- Creative BC and Canada Media Fund support for specific Vancouver indies: not researched.

## 6. The post-Covid rebuild (2022–2026)

### Takeaway
After Full Indie's hiatus, a newcomer (Nazaradeh) restarted meetups in 2022 or 2023 at The Gaming Stadium in Richmond, with 100+ people a month. The Summit returned in 2024 and grew in each of the next two years: Emily Carr in 2024, the Vancouver Playhouse in 2025, the Westin Bayshore in 2026. New grassroots showcases appeared alongside it (Vancouver Game Garden 2024–25), and IGDA Vancouver is meeting again.

### Cited Findings
- See section 3 (Nazaradeh, Gaming Stadium, the 2024–2026 Summits) and section 4 (Game Garden, IGDA 2025–26 meetings).
- Indie developers who moved from Boston to Toronto told CBC that "Vancouver is a much more vibrant city than Toronto for indie games right now." — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/arts/video-game-brain-gain-why-two-american-developers-chose-to-move-to-canada-1.7578941) (snippet; date not confirmed)

### Gaps
- Who organised in the gap between 2020 and the restart: informal Discords, other meetups?
- How the 2023 layoffs fed new indie studios, the move from The Gaming Stadium to Microsoft and LaSalle, and the handover to Chloe Ezra.

## 7. A "Vancouver style"? Culture and cost of living

### Takeaway
I could not find a published source for Greg Lobanov's "Vancouver style" theory (nature, environmentalism, emotional stories). His published quotes are about Vancouver as a hub: "biggest indie game city on the planet," "kind of a cultural centre." The theory should be treated as unsourced and confirmed in an interview. Housing and cost-of-living pressure are documented, but mostly by AAA and mid-size studios (2019).

### Cited Findings
- **Lobanov's published quotes**
  - "I do think Vancouver might be the biggest indie game city on the planet." "Vancouver was where I had the largest community of friends and other game developers that I knew of." — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
  - Indie games have made Vancouver "kind of a cultural centre." — [Georgia Straight 2025](https://www.straight.com/arts/you-nearly-missed-local-indie-games-bloom-at-vancouver-game-garden)
  - His 2015 leaving interview, on why he chose Vancouver, was not opened. — [Technical.ly Philly, May 2015](https://technical.ly/philly/2015/05/11/greg-lobanov-exit-interview)
  - Wandersong grew out of a cross-country bike trip. — [Game Pilgrim interview 2018](https://gamepilgrim.com/2018/10/21/developer-interview-greg-lobanov/)
- **Housing and cost of living**
  - Ian Verchere (Session Games): "Vancouver has real challenges with housing and cost of living," and "it's really hard to find cool spaces for developers." — [PocketGamer.biz Region Focus, 18 Jan 2019 (sponsored)](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/region-focus-vancouver/)
  - Kenshi Arasaki (A Thinking Ape): "The cost of living in Vancouver is high," which makes it hard to attract senior talent with families. — same source
  - Verchere also noted outdoor culture, with locals as "skiers, boarders and mountain bikers," and Josh Nilson (Eastside Games) called the collaboration "very Canadian." — same source
- **Indie House as a housing answer:** a shared house near Steveston (2012 to late 2016). — [Georgia Straight 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)

### Inferences
- Indie House and Lobanov moving "in with friends in a house in Richmond" suggest that shared housing outside the city core was one way indies coped with Vancouver costs. No source states that explicitly.

### Gaps
- No source for Lobanov's theory. It may come from a talk, podcast or social post. Ask him directly, with the three elements as prompts.
- No reporting from 2023–2026 on indies leaving Vancouver because of housing.
- Audio studios (A Shell in the Pit, Power Up Audio): the search turned up only a Bandcamp profile for A Shell in the Pit ([Bandcamp](https://ashellinthepit.bandcamp.com/subscribe)). Founding dates and histories are still unsourced.
