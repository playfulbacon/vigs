# Rebuilding Vancouver's Indie Games Timeline, Source by Source

Most of the draft timeline holds up. Its main problems are imprecise dates, links to mirror sites, and a few claims that are wrong or out of date. Five errors matter most. First, the *Earthblade* cancellation came from a dispute with co-founder **Pedro Medeiros** over *Celeste*'s IP, and published accounts disagree about how much that dispute caused it. Second, the **Full Indie Summit came back after Covid**: Sept 21, 2024 at Emily Carr, Sept 13, 2025 at the Vancouver Playhouse and Sept 20, 2026 at the Westin Bayshore. Third, ***Beastieball* is still in Early Access**, with 1.0 now set for early 2027. Fourth, **GDC Canada ran twice (2009 and 2010)**, not once. Fifth, the first Full Indie Summit has an exact date, **April 20, 2013**. Several "Vancouver" milestones also need a geography caveat. The Northways made *Fantastic Contraption* (2008) and *Incredipede* (2012) before they settled in Vancouver in 2014. Justin Smith's Vancouver residence is not documented in any opened source. Hinterland (*The Long Dark*) and Red Nexus (*Peglin*) are Vancouver Island and Victoria stories, so they now sit in a sidebar. The research also adds about 100 dated events the draft lacked. They include the AAA spin-off lineage (Radical 1991 → Relic 1997 → Barking Dog and Black Box 1998 → Next Level 2002 → Hothead, United Front), Red Hook's *Darkest Dungeon*, the two audio houses that link most of the scene's hits (Power Up Audio and A Shell in the Pit), the newer wave around *1000xRESIST*, and a cluster of Vancouver releases that have slipped into 2027. The scene before 2008 and the Victoria meetup said to have seeded Full Indie remain almost undocumented. Interviews will have to supply them.

## What the evidence changes about the story

The draft treats 2008 as the start of a Vancouver indie scene. The sources point somewhere slightly different. Before 2010, independent work in the region came mostly from AAA spin-offs founded by veterans of EA, Radical and Relic. **Klei (July 2005)** is the clearest early case: Jamie Cheng left Relic and bootstrapped it ([Wikipedia: Klei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment); [Georgia Straight, 2013](https://www.straight.com/life/425486/bov-2013-heres-video-games-made-vancouver)). Alongside that were solo Flash developers working outside the city. The Northways, for example, began at the University of Victoria ([Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)). The first documented organizing came on **April 24, 2010**, when UK transplant Jake Birkett, then at Big Fish Games, proposed a monthly meetup and Alex Vostrov answered the same day ([Grey Alien Games blog](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/game-industry-meetups-in-vancouver/)). That meetup became Full Indie. The house near Steveston that Thorson and Chevy Ray Johnston took around October 2012 gave the scene its physical centre until late 2016. The jam, house and audio pipeline then fed the hits of 2017 to 2021: *Night in the Woods*, *Celeste*, *Wandersong*, *Ikenfell* and *Chicory*.

Two threads run through the record. One is **Klei as a patron**: it published *Crypt of the NecroDancer* (2015), *Rift of the NecroDancer* (2025) and *Beastieball* (2024) ([Wikipedia: Klei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment)). The other is a **shared local audio supply chain**. Power Up Audio's route into games went from the 2012 Full Indie Game Jam to Indie House to *TowerFall*, and then to *NecroDancer*, *Darkest Dungeon* and *Celeste* ([EGM](https://egmnow.com/sonic-boom-how-vancouvers-power-up-audio-became-an-indie-powerhouse/)). A Shell in the Pit is credited on *Wandersong*, *Chicory*, *Beastieball*, *Ikenfell* and *Night in the Woods* ([A Shell in the Pit](https://ashellinthepit.com/)).

The current era looks different from the house-share years. Mid-size independents contracted: Phoenix Labs, Blackbird and Brace Yourself all made layoffs between 2023 and 2025. Full Indie, by contrast, came back larger than before. Its 2026 Summit had 1,351 RSVPs on Luma, against "650+" billed for 2019. That is an RSVP count, not audited attendance ([Luma](https://luma.com/avvyh3gy)). Newer teams from outside the Indie House lineage also drew critical attention, notably Sunset Visitor, whose *1000xRESIST* won a 2024 Peabody Award ([Wikipedia: 1000xResist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000xResist)).

### Confidence legend

- ✅ **Verified.** Checked against at least one opened primary source (a studio, organizer or government page, or the developer's own post) or an opened press article. Wikipedia alone does not count.
- ◐ **Single or secondary source.** Rests on one secondary source (usually a canonical en.wikipedia.org page that was opened), or on a search-result snippet where the page itself was not opened. Treat as probable, and re-check it before quoting.
- ⚠ **Disputed or conflicting.** Sources disagree. Both accounts are given.

Every link points to a canonical page: en.wikipedia.org, the publisher, or the press outlet. Mirror sites have been removed. Where a page returned 403 or could not be read, the entry says so.

## Updated timeline

### The AAA roots and first spin-offs (1982–2007)

*Era bounds: unchanged in substance. Klei (2005) stays here because it belongs to the AAA spin-off pattern, not to the meetup-driven scene that started in 2010.*

- ✅ **1982**: Don Mattrick (age 17) and Jeff Sember found **Distinctive Software Inc. (DSI)** in Burnaby, with Tarrnie Williams helping to license their first game, *Evolution*. Wikipedia's EA Vancouver lead says the studio "opened" in January 1983. Sember sold his stake to Mattrick in 1986.
  - [Georgia Straight, "Vancouver's video game family tree," Jan 28, 2009](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree) · [Wikipedia: Don Mattrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mattrick) · [Wikipedia: Distinctive Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinctive_Software) · [Wikipedia: EA Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Vancouver)
- ⚠ **June 18, 1991 (announced) / July 1991 (closed)**: EA buys DSI, which becomes **EA Canada** (later EA Vancouver) in Burnaby. Wikipedia gives two prices: **US$10M** (citing the NYT, June 18, 1991) and **$11M** (EA Vancouver history section). The NYT article could not be opened. Wikipedia's claim that it is EA's "largest and oldest studio" has no citation. A documented alternative is "one of EA's largest studios, about 1,300 staff (2013)."
  - [Wikipedia: Distinctive Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinctive_Software) · [Wikipedia: EA Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Vancouver) · [NYT, June 18, 1991 (not opened)](https://www.nytimes.com/1991/06/18/business/company-news-electronic-arts-to-buy-distinctive.html)
- ✅ **September 1991**: **Radical Entertainment** is founded by Rory Armes (ex-DSI), Dave Davis and Ian Wilkinson, first based in Yaletown.
  - [Wikipedia: Radical Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Entertainment) · [Georgia Straight family tree](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree)
- ◐ **1997**: New Media BC, the industry association later renamed DigiBC, is founded. It merged with WinBC in 2009. Source: search snippet only.
  - [DigiBC 2017 Year in Review](https://digibc.org/?p=8144)
- ✅ **May 1997**: **Relic Entertainment** is founded by Alex Garden, Luke Moloney, Erin Daly, Aaron Kambeitz, Rob Cunningham, Gary Shaw and Shane Alfreds. The Straight names Garden and Moloney as coming "from EA Canada/Radical."
  - [Wikipedia: Relic Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic_Entertainment) · [Georgia Straight family tree](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree)
- ◐ **May 1998**: Six ex-Radical developers (Glenn Barnes, Peter Grant, Michael Gyori, Christopher Mair, Brian Thalken and Sean Thompson) found **Barking Dog Studios**. In the same year, other ex-Radical staff found **Black Box Games** in Burnaby.
  - [Wikipedia: Rockstar Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Vancouver) · [Wikipedia: EA Black Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Black_Box)
- ◐ **September 28, 1999**: Relic releases ***Homeworld***.
  - [Wikipedia: Relic Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic_Entertainment)
- ◐ **2002 consolidation**: In June, EA buys Black Box, which becomes EA Black Box. On August 1, Take-Two buys Barking Dog for US$3M and it becomes **Rockstar Vancouver**. On August 22, ex-Black Box staff Eric Randall, Douglas Tronsgard, Jason Carr and David Catlin found **Next Level Games**.
  - [Wikipedia: EA Black Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Black_Box) · [Wikipedia: Rockstar Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Vancouver) · [Wikipedia: Next Level Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Level_Games)
- ◐ **April 27, 2004**: THQ announces it will acquire Relic for about US$10M.
  - [Wikipedia: Relic Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic_Entertainment)
- ◐ **2004–2005 spin-off wave**: **Blue Castle Games** is founded in Burnaby on July 4, 2004 by Robert Barrett, and later becomes Capcom Vancouver. **Propaganda Games** is founded in April 2005 as Revolution Interactive by Josh Holmes, Howard Donaldson, Daryl Anselmo and Jorge Freitas, and Disney buys it the same year. **Slant Six Games** is founded in 2005 with Barking Dog co-founder Brian Thalken.
  - [Wikipedia: Capcom Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom_Vancouver) · [Wikipedia: Propaganda Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Games) · [Wikipedia: Slant Six Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Six_Games)
- ✅ **July 2005**: **Jamie Cheng**, who had been a specialist AI programmer at Relic, bootstraps **Klei Entertainment**. Jeffrey Agala is the documented co-founder and creative director. ◐ Wikipedia adds that Cheng funded it by selling his THQ shares and borrowing $10,000 from his brother. The July month also rests on Wikipedia.
  - [Georgia Straight, "BOV 2013," Sept 18, 2013](https://www.straight.com/life/425486/bov-2013-heres-video-games-made-vancouver) · [Wikipedia: Klei Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment)
- ◐ **March 27, 2006**: Klei's first game, ***Eets***, comes out for Windows.
  - [Wikipedia: Klei Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment) · [Road to the IGF: Eets](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-klei-entertainment-s-i-eets-i-)
- ✅ **2006**: **Action Pants** is founded. It later becomes Ubisoft Vancouver.
  - [Game Informer, Jan 17, 2012](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2012/01/17/ubisoft-closes-vancouver-studio)
- ◐ **2006**: Ex-Radical staff Steve Bocska, Vlad Ceraldi and Joel DeYoung found **Hothead Games**. Its first releases are the *Penny Arcade Adventures* episodes (2008).
  - [Wikipedia: Hothead Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hothead_Games)
- ◐ **October 17, 2006**: Rockstar Vancouver ships ***Bully*** on PS2 in North America. It reaches Europe on October 25 as *Canis Canem Edit*.
  - [Wikipedia: Bully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bully_(video_game)) · [Wikipedia: Rockstar Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Vancouver)
- ✅ **2007**: **United Front Games** is founded by about 10 veterans of EA Black Box, Rockstar Vancouver, Radical and others.
  - [Wikipedia: United Front Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_Games) · [Georgia Straight family tree](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree)
- ◐ **2007**: Relic founding members Rob Cunningham and Aaron Kambeitz, with other ex-Relic and ex-EA staff, found **Blackbird Interactive**. An earlier brief gave 2010; Wikipedia says 2007.
  - [Wikipedia: Blackbird Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbird_Interactive)
- ◐ **Before 2009 (years unknown)**: Vancouver hosts the **Vancouver International Game Summit**, the industry conference that came before GDC Canada.
  - [Game Developer: GDC Canada 2009 round-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-canada-2009-the-event-round-up)

### The first indies (2008–2011)

- ✅ **September 16, 2008**: **Colin Northway** releases the Flash game ***Fantastic Contraption***. He made it on the side while building websites for lawyers. It drew more than 3.5M unique users in about three months, and he sold the rights to inXile (iOS release Jan 26, 2009). **Geography caveat:** the Northways began at the University of Victoria and then travelled for about five years. Where Colin lived in 2008 is undocumented.
  - [Georgia Straight, Mar 27, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games) · [Wikipedia: Fantastic Contraption (2008)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Contraption_(2008_video_game)) · [Game Developer: GDC Austin 2009 talk (snippet)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-austin-how-i-fantastic-contraption-i-became-a-fantastic-hit)
- ✅ **September and December 2008**: The downturn reaches Vancouver. Backbone Entertainment cuts most of its Vancouver staff in September, and Hothead cuts staff in December after failing to find a publisher for *Swarm*.
  - [Georgia Straight family tree](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree)
- ✅ **January 28, 2009**: Blaine Kyllo's "**Vancouver's video game family tree**" (Georgia Straight) maps the spin-offs from EA and Radical, and also the 2008 layoffs. Alex Garden is quoted: "There's probably 50 companies that have started up and failed that I've never even heard of."
  - [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/article-198534/video-game-family-tree)
- ✅ **January 30 – February 1, 2009**: The first **Global Game Jam** has a Vancouver site at SFU Harbour Centre, organized through IGDA, with 26 entrants.
  - [Village Gamer, Feb 3, 2009](https://www.villagegamer.net/2009/02/03/global-game-jam-vancouver-creativity-in-a-box/)
- ✅ **February 2009**: Ubisoft acquires Action Pants.
  - [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2012/01/17/ubisoft-closes-vancouver-studio)
- ◐ **2009**: The first **GDC Canada** is held at the Vancouver Convention Centre, with more than 800 attendees. It is produced by Think Services and Reboot Communications.
  - [Game Developer: GDC Canada 2009 round-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-canada-2009-the-event-round-up)
- ◐ **April 4, 2009**: **Justin Smith** releases ***Enviro-Bear 2000*** on Game Jolt. He made it for the TIGSource Cockpit Competition, drew the art in MS Paint, and it won the most user votes. The iOS version, *Enviro-Bear 2010*, follows on July 8, 2009. **Geography caveat:** the opened sources call Smith "Canadian" without naming a city.
  - [Wikipedia: Enviro-Bear 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enviro-Bear_2000)
- ✅ **January 12, 2010**: Game Developer runs a "Road to the IGF" feature on *Enviro-Bear 2000*, a **2010 IGF Nuovo Award finalist**.
  - [Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-justin-smith-s-i-enviro-bear-2000-i-) · [Game Developer, "Bear Theft Auto," Nov 19, 2010](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-bear-theft-auto---the-indie-adventures-of-i-enviro-bear-i-s-justin-smith)
- ✅ **January 2010**: Global Game Jam Vancouver moves to UBC under Kimberly Voll of the Centre for Digital Media, who has run it since.
  - [Georgia Straight, Jan 24, 2013](https://www.straight.com/life/345091/global-game-jam-enthusiasm-keeps-growing)
- ✅ **February 2010 (announced) / September 1, 2010 (in effect)**: BC introduces the **Interactive Digital Media Tax Credit**, a 17.5% credit on BC labour costs for projects starting after Aug 31, 2010. DigiBC is credited with lobbying for it.
  - [Georgia Straight, 2010](https://www.straight.com/blogra/bc-video-game-industry-gets-some-support-new-175-percent-labour-tax-credit) · [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/film-media-tax-credits/british-columbia-refundable-media-tax-credits.html)
- ✅ **April 24 – May 22, 2010**: **Jake Birkett** (Big Fish Games, a UK transplant) and **Alex Vostrov** (Rocket Bear Games, formerly Indiebird) start the **Vancouver Indie Game Developers** meetup. Birkett proposes it on April 24, and they set up the meetup.com group by May 10. The first meeting is at Soho Cafe and Billiards in Yaletown, with 40+ attendees from 60+ members; Birkett wrote it up on May 22. ⚠ **Victoria roots:** Sarah Northway says a former Victoria roommate "started a meet-up in Victoria for indie devs" and "brought it with him." Birkett's accounts do not mention Victoria. See the sidebar.
  - [Grey Alien Games blog, Apr 24, 2010](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/game-industry-meetups-in-vancouver/) · [Grey Alien Games blog, first meetup](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/first-vancouver-indie-game-developer-meetup-was-a-big-success/) · [Full Indie: About](https://www.fullindie.com/about) · [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- ✅ **May 6–7, 2010**: **GDC Canada** returns to the Vancouver Convention Centre for its second edition and offers free passes to laid-off developers.
  - [Newswire](https://newswire.ca/news-releases/canadian-game-development-community-to-reconvene-at-the-second-annual-gamedevelopers-conferencetm-canada-may-6-7-2010-539206541.html) · [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/2010-gdc-canada-offers-free-passes-for-laid-off-developers)
- ✅ **July 19, 2010**: Game Developer profiles Vostrov ("I'm from Vancouver, Canada") as he goes full-time indie.
  - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-a-rocket-bear-takes-off---vostrov-on-going-full-time-indie)
- ✅ **2010**: Klei ships ***Shank***. It had earlier declined to make *N+* for Toronto's Metanet, though it is credited with engine work on it.
  - [CGM, History of the Canadian Videogame Scene Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/) · [Wikipedia: Klei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment)
- ◐ **2010**: Amora Bettany, Pedro Medeiros and Heidy Motta found **MiniBoss** in São Paulo. The studio later did art for *TowerFall* and *Celeste*.
  - [Wikipedia: Amora Bettany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amora_Bettany)
- ✅ **January 2011**: Disney closes **Propaganda Games**. Around the same time, *True Crime: Hong Kong* is cancelled.
  - [Georgia Straight, Aug 31, 2011](https://www.straight.com/node/24485) · [Wikipedia: Propaganda Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Games)
- ◐ **March 2011**: After nine meetings, the group rebrands as **Full Indie**, launches FullIndie.com and moves to a venue for about 100 people. Su Skerl and Jason Elliot design the brand. The month comes from CGM alone.
  - [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/) · [Full Indie: About](https://www.fullindie.com/about) · [Wikipedia: Full Indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie)
- ✅ **May 10, 2011**: The Straight reports that GDC Canada "seems to be finished" after two years and is being replaced by the Canadian Games Conference.
  - [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/node/44385)
- ✅ **May 30, 2011**: The Entertainment Software Association of Canada (ESAC), citing a Secor Consulting report, counts about 16,000 game jobs in Canada at nearly 350 companies, with Quebec holding over half. ◐ Figures quoted elsewhere put BC at 3,882 employees in 83 companies (snippet only).
  - [ESAC](https://theesa.ca/?p=2077)
- ◐ **June 2011 (weekend of June 17)**: The first **Full Indie Game Jam** is held at the Centre for Digital Media, with 30 spots. Source: search snippet of Village Gamer only. Global Game Jams had already run in Vancouver in 2009, 2010 and 2011, so CGM's "first two" Global Game Jams is loose wording.
  - [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/)
- ✅ **August 31, 2011**: The Straight's "Vancouver video-game industry in transition" reports that "Montreal has taken the lead when it comes to employment" (ESAC's Julien Lavoie). East Side Games' Jason Bailey says momentum is in social and mobile.
  - [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/node/24485)
- ◐ **2011**: Sarah Northway releases ***Rebuild*** and ***Rebuild 2***. Northway Games' press kit gives 2011 as the studio's founding year.
  - [Wikipedia: Northway Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northway_Games) · [Northway Games press kit](https://northwaygames.com/presskit/)

### The Indie House era (2012–2016)

- ✅ **January 17, 2012**: Ubisoft closes **Ubisoft Vancouver** (formerly Action Pants, founded 2006, acquired 2009). Staff are offered positions at other Ubisoft studios.
  - [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2012/01/17/ubisoft-closes-vancouver-studio)
- ✅ **June 2012**: ***TowerFall*** starts as a single-player Flash prototype at the 48-hour **Full Indie Game Jam**. The idea came from Maddy Thorson's visit with Alec Holowka at the jam. Holowka stepped back from the project soon after.
  - [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/) · [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)
- ◐ **June 2012**: At the same jam, Kevin Regamey of **Power Up Audio** meets Noel Berry and Chevy Ray Johnston. The TowerFall team later invites PUA to Indie House.
  - [EGM: "Sonic Boom"](https://egmnow.com/sonic-boom-how-vancouvers-power-up-audio-became-an-indie-powerhouse/)
- ◐ **June 28, 2012**: Activision announces a "significant reduction in staff" at Radical, which becomes a support studio.
  - [Wikipedia: Radical Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Entertainment)
- ◐ **July 2012**: Rockstar merges **Rockstar Vancouver** into Rockstar Toronto, announced July 9. Its 35 staff are offered relocation. In the same month EA Black Box is renamed Quicklime Games, and it closes in April 2013.
  - [Wikipedia: Rockstar Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Vancouver) · [Wikipedia: EA Black Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Black_Box)
- ◐ **2012**: Gamasutra reports Vancouver talent leaving for tax incentives in Montreal and Toronto.
  - [Game Developer: "What's really going down in Vancouver"](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/what-s-really-going-down-in-vancouver-)
- ◐ **September 7, 2012**: Klei releases ***Mark of the Ninja*** on Xbox Live Arcade, published by Microsoft Studios (PC on Oct 16). Nels Anderson is lead designer.
  - [Wikipedia: Mark of the Ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_of_the_Ninja) · [Vancouver Weekly, Apr 25, 2013](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)
- ◐ **October 9, 2012**: **Brian Provinciano** (Vblank Entertainment) releases ***Retro City Rampage*** on PC, PS3 and Vita. Vancouver Weekly later calls him "one of the biggest local names."
  - [Wikipedia: Retro City Rampage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro_City_Rampage) · [Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)
- ✅ **c. October 2012 – late 2016**: **Indie House** is "a literal house near Steveston" in Richmond, filled with indie developers. Chevy Ray Johnston suggested getting a house. Documented residents are Maddy Thorson, Chevy Ray Johnston, Alec Holowka, Noel Berry and (from 2015) Greg Lobanov. At one point four of its seven residents were working on *TowerFall*. The October 2012 month comes from Wikipedia (◐).
  - [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games) · [Wikipedia: Maddy Thorson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson) · [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)
- ✅ **October 25, 2012**: The Northways release ***Incredipede***, designed by Colin and Sarah Northway with art by Thomas Shahan. It is a 2013 IGF finalist. It was **made while travelling**, before they settled in Vancouver.
  - [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games) · [Wikipedia: Incredipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredipede)
- ◐ **November 2012**: Thorson incorporates her company, Matt Makes Games Inc. It is later renamed Maddy Makes Games.
  - [Wikipedia: Maddy Thorson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson)
- ✅ **November 30, 2012**: Full Indie has "well over 1000 members and monthly meetups with over 100 people." ◐ Birkett leaves Vancouver in 2012, and a UK branch, FullIndie.uk (formerly Best of British), starts the same year.
  - [Grey Alien Games, "My Game Dev Story"](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/?p=1044) · [Full Indie: About](https://www.fullindie.com/about) · [Wikipedia: Full Indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie)

  - [Wikipedia: Darkest Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon) · [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)

### Sidebar: Vancouver Island and Victoria

These events are BC-scene context. They are not Vancouver claims, and the main timeline keeps them out.

- ✅ **2000s**: Colin and Sarah Northway start making indie games while at the **University of Victoria**.
  - [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- ◐ **c. 2010 or earlier**: A former Victoria roommate of the Northways "started a meet-up in Victoria for indie devs, and when he moved to Vancouver, I think he brought it with him" (Sarah Northway). He is not named. The Straight treats this as a Full Indie origin. Birkett's contemporary posts call Vostrov "another Vancouver resident" and do not mention Victoria.
  - [Georgia Straight, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games) · [Grey Alien Games blog](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/first-vancouver-indie-game-developer-meetup-was-a-big-success/)
- ◐ **September – October 2013**: Ex-Relic developer **Raphael van Lierop** moves from Vancouver to the **Comox Valley** and forms **Hinterland Studio** as a mostly remote team. ***The Long Dark*** is announced and Kickstarted with Canada Media Fund seed money.
  - [Wikipedia: The Long Dark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Dark)
- ◐ **September 2014 / August 1, 2017**: *The Long Dark* enters Early Access in 2014 and gets its full release in 2017. It sells 3.3M copies by September 2019 and 5M by 2021. Ports: Switch (Sept 17, 2020), Xbox Series (June 19, 2025) and PS5 (Nov 11, 2025). The final Wintermute episode comes out on March 30, 2026.
  - [Wikipedia: The Long Dark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Dark)
- ⚠ **2015 or 2016**: **Dylan Gedig** founds **Red Nexus Games**, which has University of Victoria roots. The founding year differs between sources.
  - [Shacknews (snippet)](https://shacknews.com/article/127914/peglin-developer-talks-making-the-game-with-a-four-person-team)
- ◐ **2019 → August 27, 2024**: ***Peglin*** starts at **Orca Jam 2019**. It enters Steam Early Access on April 25, 2022, sells about 400K Early Access copies in its first year, and reaches 1.0 with a Switch release on August 27, 2024.
  - [Wikipedia: Peglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peglin) · [Game Developer press release](https://gamedeveloper.com/press-release/from-game-jam-to-winning-best-design-the-story-behind-peglin)
- ◐ **December 12, 2024 → 2027**: Hinterland announces ***Blackfrost: The Long Dark 2*** at TGA 2024, with Early Access planned for 2026. It later slips to 2027 (PC Gamer, per search summary), and Steam lists "To be announced." ◐ Hinterland shifts developers back to *The Long Dark* DLC "to survive an uncertain industry."
  - [Wikipedia: The Long Dark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Dark) · [PC Gamer (snippet)](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/the-long-dark-will-get-new-dlc-again-as-studio-shifts-devs-away-from-the-sequel-to-survive-an-uncertain-industry-we-have-to-adapt-to-this/)
- ◐ **Today**: An **IGDA Victoria** chapter exists. Its founding date is unknown.
  - [IGDA Victoria on itch.io](https://igdavictoria.itch.io)

## Key people

*Cards record only documented facts. A birth date appears only where a source gives one. Pronouns appear only where publicly stated. Anything not stated in a source is marked "not found."*

**Don Mattrick.** Born Feb 13, 1964, in Vancouver ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mattrick)). SFU; co-founded DSI in 1982 at 17. Later president of EA Worldwide Studios, head of Xbox (2007–2013) and CEO of Zynga (2013–2015). **Status:** co-president of MdGB Capital; Order of British Columbia (2024). A figure from the AAA prehistory, not an indie participant.

**Jeff Sember.** Co-founded DSI (1982) and sold his stake to Mattrick in 1986 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mattrick)). Birth year, later career and status: not found.

**Jamie Cheng.** SFU computer science; AI programmer at Relic for nearly three years (◐). Founded Klei in July 2005 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment)). Klei games: *Shank*, *Mark of the Ninja*, *Don't Starve*, *Oxygen Not Included*, *Griftlands*, *Rotwood*. Under him Klei published Vancouver peers. **Status:** CEO as of the most recent source (2017 context). Not confirmed for 2026.

**Jeffrey Agala.** Klei co-founder and creative director ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klei_Entertainment)). Nothing further found.

**Nels Anderson.** Lead designer of *Mark of the Ninja* (Klei). Spoke at the 2013 Summit, and at the 2016 Summit as a *Firewatch* developer ([Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)). Current status: not found.

**Colin and Sarah Northway.** Began making games at the University of Victoria. Colin built websites for lawyers; Sarah worked in game development in San Francisco. They travelled for about five years and settled in Vancouver in 2014 ([Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)). Colin's games: *Fantastic Contraption* (2008, VR 2016), *Incredipede* (2012), *Deep Under the Sky*. Sarah's games: the *Rebuild* series, and *I Was a Teenage Exocolonist* (2022) as designer ([Wikipedia: Northway Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northway_Games)). **Status:** Northway Games is active. Colin's site says he has returned to the studio and both are working on unannounced games; the press kit names Sarah as the sole active member, and the two statements may be from different dates. Likely witnesses to the Victoria meetup, and hosts who drew Davey Wreden to the city.

**Justin Smith.** Canadian solo developer. Made *Enviro-Bear 2000* (2009; IGF 2010 Nuovo finalist), *Desert Golfing* (2014, Blinkbat Games) and *Golf on Mars* and *Horsey Game* (2026) as Captain Games ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Golfing); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3602570/Horsey_Game/)). His Vancouver residence is unconfirmed (a Medium profile says "Vancouver-based" but was not read). Birth year: not found. Notably low-profile.

**Jake Birkett.** UK developer brought to Vancouver by Big Fish Games, where he worked for about two years. Proposed and co-founded the meetup that became Full Indie in 2010, and left Vancouver in 2012 ([Grey Alien Games](https://greyaliengames.com/blog/?p=1044); [Full Indie](https://www.fullindie.com/about)). Runs Grey Alien Games (*Regency Solitaire*, *Shadowhand*, *Ancient Enemy*). **Status:** UK-based. That rests on a Wikipedia mirror; confirm with him directly.

**Alex Vostrov.** Vancouver solo developer at Rocket Bear Games, formerly Indiebird (*Infested Planet*) ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-a-rocket-bear-takes-off---vostrov-on-going-full-time-indie)). Full Indie co-founder and president 2010–2016 ([Full Indie](https://www.fullindie.com/about)). Possibly the Victoria roommate in Sarah Northway's account; this is unconfirmed.

**Maurice Grela.** Full Indie president from 2016 to about 2023 ([Full Indie](https://www.fullindie.com/about)). Nothing further found.

**Aryo Nazaradeh.** Returned to Vancouver from Toronto in 2022. Got involved through game audio and networking, restarted meetups (at The Gaming Stadium in Richmond by 2024), and became Full Indie president ([Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)). **Status:** no longer listed as president. The handover date to Chloe Ezra is unknown.

**Chloe Ezra.** Current Full Indie president ([Full Indie](https://www.fullindie.com/about)).

**Brian Provinciano.** Vblank Entertainment. *Retro City Rampage* (Oct 9, 2012). Conceived the first Full Indie Summit (2013) ([Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-10-full-indie-summit-brings-vancouver-dev-industry-together.html)).

**Kimberly Voll.** Centre for Digital Media. Has run Global Game Jam Vancouver since 2010 ([Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/life/345091/global-game-jam-enthusiasm-keeps-growing)).

**Maddy Thorson** (she/her, per her Mastodon, cited by Wikipedia). Born March 18, 1988, per Wikipedia citing her own 2009 blog post ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson)). Studied at Grande Prairie Regional College in Alberta, then moved into a Vancouver apartment with Chevy Ray Johnston; the arrival year was not found. Indie House resident. Lead creator of *TowerFall* (2013) and *Celeste* (2018). Founded Matt Makes Games Inc. (Nov 2012), later Maddy Makes Games and then EXOK (2019). Forbes 30 Under 30 (2014). **Status:** runs EXOK with Noel Berry after *Earthblade* (cancelled Jan 2025). Released the *Masterpiece* romhack (2025).

**Chevy Ray Johnston.** Thorson's roommate after college. Suggested Indie House. Lead developer, artist and writer of *Ikenfell* (2020, Happy Ray Games) ([Wikipedia: Ikenfell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikenfell)). Pronouns and birth year: not found. A key early witness.

**Noel Berry.** Indie House resident. *Skytorn* (announced 2014, cancelled 2018). Co-made *Celeste Classic* and was lead programmer on *Celeste* ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2014/12/06/procedurally-generated-adventure-skytorn-announced-ps4/)). **Status:** co-leads EXOK. Making *City of None* with his brother Liam for 2027 ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/04/30/city-of-none-is-a-metroidvania-from-a-celeste-co-creator-and-it-launches-next-year)).

**Amora Bettany.** Brazilian artist, born 1987 in São Paulo, per Wikipedia citing her own site ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amora_Bettany)). Co-founded MiniBoss (2010). Moved to Vancouver in 2019. Credits: *TowerFall*, *Celeste*, *Celeste 64* and *Wanderstop* concept art. **Status:** Vancouver-based; left EXOK around the IP dispute (per Wikipedia). Current project: not found.

**Pedro Medeiros ("saint11").** Brazilian pixel artist; co-founded MiniBoss and EXOK. Art on *TowerFall* and *Celeste*. Left EXOK in Nov 2024 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/extremely-ok-games-has-cancelled-its-follow-up-to-celeste-000352550.html)). **Status:** co-runs Coldblood Inc., which Gematsu (snippet) calls Vancouver-based; *Neverway* is due in 2027. His own residence is unconfirmed.

**Heidy Motta.** MiniBoss co-founder and EXOK operations manager. Her tweet supplied the "Extremely OK Games" name ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson)).

**Lena Raine.** Born Feb 29, 1984, in Seattle, per Wikipedia citing her own tweet ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_Raine)). Composer of *Celeste*, *Chicory* and *Beastieball*; founding EXOK team member. Also *Minecraft* and *Deltarune* work. **Vancouver residence: unconfirmed.** List her as a collaborator.

**Alec Holowka.** Born Oct 30, 1983, in Winnipeg; died Aug 31, 2019, in Winnipeg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Holowka)). Co-founded Bit Blot (*Aquaria*, IGF Grand Prize), Infinite Ammo and Infinite Fall. Early career on an Xbox 360 racer in Vancouver. At the June 2012 jam that produced *TowerFall*, and lived at Indie House (per Wikipedia: TowerFall). By the IGF period he was working on *Night in the Woods* remotely from Winnipeg. **2019:** publicly accused of abuse by Zoë Quinn in August 2019. The *Night in the Woods* team cut ties the next day, and he died four days after the allegations. See the timeline entry for the attributed statements. His Vancouver residence dates are unknown.

**Scott Benson and Bethany Hockenberry.** *Night in the Woods* co-creators, based in Pittsburgh ([IGF](https://igf.com/night-woods)). Not Vancouver residents. Benson's The Glory Society closed in Nov 2023 after his heart-disease diagnosis ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Benson)).

**Greg Lobanov.** Born and raised in Philadelphia; Drexel graduate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Lobanov)). Age 22 in May 2015 ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/philly/2015/05/11/greg-lobanov-exit-interview/)); no birth year is recorded. Moved into the Richmond house in 2015. Earlier work as Dumb and Fat Games. Games: *Wandersong* (2018), *Chicory* (2021, BAFTA) and *Beastieball* (Early Access 2024) at Wishes Unlimited. Co-organizes Vancouver Game Garden and leads a developer volleyball team. **Status:** active; *Beastieball* 1.0 due early 2027.

**Alexis Dean-Jones.** Lead artist on *Chicory* and art on *Beastieball* ([Wikipedia: Chicory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicory:_A_Colorful_Tale)). Residence not established.

**Ryan Clark.** Founded Brace Yourself Games in Vancouver (2013). Director of *Crypt of the NecroDancer* and *Cadence of Hyrule* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brace_Yourself_Games)). Birth year and pre-2013 career: not found.

**Daniel Mullins.** Daniel Mullins Games: *Pony Island* (2016), *The Hex* (2018) and *Inscryption* (2021; IGF Grand Prize and GDCA Game of the Year). Described as Vancouver-based ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/magic-myst-pokemon-inspire-inscryption/)). ◐ Previously a programmer at Vancouver studios. **Status:** *Pony Island 2* dated Apr 27, 2027 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/pony-island-2-panda-circus-launches-on-april-27-2027/)).

**Gordon McGladdery.** Founder and composer of A Shell in the Pit. Started in games on *Rogue Legacy*; game sound since 2012 per the Straight ([ASITP Press](https://ashellinthepit.com/press)). Audio on *Fantastic Contraption* VR.

Tangsoc was on stage for *Into the Breach* at BAFTA 2019. ◐ All of this rests on the single EGM feature; PUA's own team page returned 404.

## Conclusion

The verification changes the shape of the oral history more than it changes the facts. Most dates check out, but the scene's origin story belongs to two places at once. It is partly a Victoria and travelling story (the Northways and the unnamed roommate) and partly a Vancouver story of transplants: Birkett from the UK, Lobanov from Philadelphia, Wreden from the US, Bettany and Medeiros from Brazil. The scene's turning points also cluster around small shared infrastructure rather than big releases: one meetup thread, one jam weekend in June 2012, one Richmond house and two audio studios. That makes the people who ran that infrastructure (Vostrov, Grela, Voll, Provinciano, Regamey and McGladdery) as important to interview as the famous designers. The record is also thinnest exactly where an oral history adds most. Indie House residency, the 2020–2023 gap and the *Earthblade* split exist mainly as single quotes or competing press framings. With *Beastieball* 1.0, *Pony Island 2*, *Neverway*, *City of None* and *Blackfrost* all slipping into 2027, the project is documenting a scene that is mid-chapter, not one that has finished.
