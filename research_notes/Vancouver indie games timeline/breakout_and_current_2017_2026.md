# Vancouver Indie Games Timeline: Fact-check of "The breakout years (2017–2021)" and "The current era (2022–2026)"

Scope: verify every claim and link in those two sections of the draft (`27587a9d-vancouver-indie-timeline.md`), swap mirror links for canonical ones, and add missed events. The research date is November 7, 2026. "Opened" means the page was fetched and checked during this research. "Search-only" means the claim rests on a search-result snippet; the page itself wasn't opened.

**Headline corrections**
1. **Earthblade IP dispute.** The dispute was NOT between Maddy Thorson and Noel Berry. Thorson and Berry were on one side and EXOK co-founder **Pedro Medeiros** was on the other. Medeiros left EXOK in November 2024, and Thorson and Berry made the call to cancel together.
2. **Earthblade first announced.** It was announced in **April 2021** and formally shown at The Game Awards 2022. The draft's "shown at TGA 2022" is correct, but it hides the 2021 announcement.
3. **Full Indie Summit.** It DID return after Covid: Sept 21, 2024 (Emily Carr University), Sept 13, 2025 (Vancouver Playhouse), and Sept 20, 2026 (Westin Bayshore). The draft's 2025 entry leaves this out. The Georgia Straight's March 2024 line that it "did not restart" was true only at the time it was written.
4. **Beastieball 1.0.** It has NOT shipped as of Nov 2026. Steam and the developer say "early 2027."
5. **DigiBC link `?p=10851`.** It is the "Full Indie Meetup: September 2025" post, which returns 403 to bots. Use the dated permalink or the Luma event page instead.
6. **Mirror links to replace.** Use https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beastieball in place of the wiki.krisyotam.com link. Use EXOK, Engadget, Wikipedia or Game Developer in place of Notebookcheck.

## Q1. Night in the Woods (2017): date and Vancouver connection

### Takeaway
**Confirmed with a nuance.** It released Feb 21, 2017. The Vancouver link is Indie House (Richmond), where part of the team worked. Night in the Woods (NITW) is not a Vancouver studio game: Infinite Fall's members were spread out, and the publisher, Finji, is US-based.

### Cited Findings
- Released **February 21, 2017** (NA) on Windows/macOS/Linux/PS4. Developed by Infinite Fall with Secret Lab (narrative engine) and published by **Finji**. Designers were Alec Holowka, Scott Benson and Bethany Hockenberry — [Wikipedia: Night in the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods) (opened)
- Kickstarter announced Oct 22, 2013 with a $50,000 goal, which it met in 26 hours. It eventually raised over US$200,000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods) (opened)
- Won the **Seumas McNally Grand Prize at IGF 2018** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods) (opened)
- The Georgia Straight (V. S. Wells, **March 27, 2024**, "How Vancouver became a hub for indie video games") says Indie House, near Steveston, ran from 2012 to late 2016 and that *Celeste*, *Night in the Woods* and *Ikenfell* came out of it — [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/node/1442795) (opened; same article as /tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- Wikipedia does not mention Vancouver or Indie House — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods) (opened)

### Inferences
- The draft's wording, "partly made at Indie House," is fair. The more precise date is "Feb 21, 2017." The † on the Wikipedia link can be removed, since the page is now verified.

### Gaps
- No source found that names which NITW team member lived or worked at Indie House, or for how long. This is a good question for interviews.

## Q2. Celeste (2018): date, team, sales, awards; Extremely OK Games formation

### Takeaway
**Confirmed / refined.** Celeste released Jan 25, 2018 under Maddy Makes Games. The 1.7 million figure is the most recent one found, from January 2025. Extremely OK Games (EXOK) was technically founded in **March 2019** and announced on **Sept 5, 2019**, not in 2018.

### Cited Findings
- Released **January 25, 2018** on PC, Switch and PS4. Xbox One followed on **Jan 26, 2018** and Stadia on Jul 28, 2020. The developer/publisher is **Maddy Makes Games** — [Wikipedia: Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) (opened)
- Credits: director/writer Maddy Thorson; programmers Thorson and Noel Berry; artists Noel Berry, **Amora Bettany**, Gabby DaRienzo and **Pedro Medeiros**; composer **Lena Raine**; sound designer Kevin Regamey — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) (opened). Kevin Regamey is a Power Up Audio co-founder. Wikipedia credits him by name, not Power Up Audio as a company.
- Origins: a 4-day PICO-8 jam with Noel Berry in **August 2015**, with full development from about January 2016 — [Wikipedia: Extremely OK Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_OK_Games) (opened)
- Sales: **500,000+ by end of 2018**, **1 million by March 2020**, **1.7 million by January 2025** — [Wikipedia: Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) (opened). The 1.7M figure appears in Earthblade-cancellation coverage. The PC Gamer article the draft cites ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/celeste-developer-starts-2025-with-a-huge-heartbreaking-and-yet-relieving-failure-as-creative-difficulties-and-internal-conflict-lead-to-the-cancellation-of-earthblade/)) resolves, but the fetch was truncated after the headline, so the 1.7M figure could not be confirmed on that page directly.
- The Game Awards 2018: Celeste **won Best Independent Game and Games for Impact** and was nominated for Game of the Year and Best Score/Music — [Wikipedia: The Game Awards 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2018) (opened); [Wikipedia: Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) (opened)
- The free **Farewell** chapter released **Sept 9, 2019** — [Wikipedia: Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) (opened)
- EXOK was "technically founded in March 2019." Its first six months went to relocating international developers to Canada. On **Sept 5, 2019** Thorson announced that Maddy Makes Games was winding down and the team was continuing as EXOK in new Vancouver offices — [Wikipedia: Extremely OK Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_OK_Games) (opened)
- Related releases: *Celeste Classic 2: Lani's Trek* (2021), *Celeste 64: Fragments of the Mountain* (2024), and the Super Mario World romhack *Masterpiece* (2025) — [Wikipedia: EXOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_OK_Games) (opened)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "**Jan 25, 2018** — *Celeste* (Maddy Makes Games), winner of Best Independent Game at The Game Awards 2018. 1.7M copies sold by Jan 2025." The Celeste Wikipedia link can drop the †.
- Pedro Medeiros is Brazilian. EXOK relocating "international developers to Canada" in 2019 likely refers to him, among others, but no source states this explicitly.

### Gaps
- No post-2025 Celeste sales figure was found.
- Power Up Audio's company credit could not be confirmed from opened sources, only Kevin Regamey's individual credit.

## Q9. Earthblade cancellation (Jan 2025) and team status since

### Takeaway
**Corrected.** EXOK announced the cancellation on **Jan 22, 2025** ("Final Earthblade Update"), with the decision made in Dec 2024. The IP dispute over Celeste was between Thorson and Berry on one side and co-founder **Pedro Medeiros** on the other, NOT between Thorson and Berry. Medeiros left in Nov 2024 and now co-runs **Coldblood Inc.** (Vancouver), whose *Neverway* has slipped from an October 2026 window to a Steam-listed 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Thorson posted the update on Jan 22, 2025. "The conflict centered around a disagreement about the IP rights of Celeste." The parties "reached a resolution" but agreed to "go our separate ways." Thorson and Noel Berry decided to cancel in December. They plan smaller-scale projects — [Shacknews, Jan 22 2025](https://www.shacknews.com/article/142820/extremely-ok-games-cancels-earthblade) (opened)
- Engadget (Anna Washenko, Jan 22, 2025): the pressure of Celeste's success made the work "so exhausting." Medeiros "parted ways with EXOK in November," and the split was described as amicable. Thorson voiced support for his new project, *Neverway*. Engadget frames it as the split "not [being] the reason" — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/extremely-ok-games-has-cancelled-its-follow-up-to-celeste-000352550.html) (opened)
