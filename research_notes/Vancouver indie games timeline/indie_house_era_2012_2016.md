## Entry 1: Jan 2012, Ubisoft closes its Vancouver studio (formerly Action Pants)

### Takeaway
**Confirmed.** Ubisoft announced the closure on **January 17, 2012**. Action Pants was founded in 2006 and bought by Ubisoft in 2009 (February 2009 per Wikipedia's Ubisoft studio table).

### Cited Findings
- Game Informer (Jim Reilly, Jan 17, 2012): "Ubisoft said today it has closed its Vancouver development studio." The studio "was previously known as Action Pants before being acquired by Ubisoft in 2009. Action Pants was first formed in 2006." Its games included *Pure Futbol*, *Academy of Champions: Soccer* (Wii) and *MotionSports Adrenaline*. Staff were offered positions at other Ubisoft studios. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2012/01/17/ubisoft-closes-vancouver-studio)
- The Wikipedia Ubisoft studio table lists Ubisoft Vancouver as founded 2006, acquired February 2009, closed January 2012. It cites Kotaku, "Ubisoft Acquires Action Pants" (Feb 3, 2009), and Gamasutra, "Ubisoft shuts down its Vancouver studio" (Tom Curtis, Jan 17, 2012). — [Wikipedia: Ubisoft (studio list)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft_Vancouver)

### Inferences
- The draft wording is accurate. A more precise version: "17 Jan 2012: Ubisoft closes Ubisoft Vancouver (Action Pants, founded 2006, acquired 2009)."

### Gaps
- The BIV link returns 403 to automated requests, so it wasn't verified. I didn't find a staff head count.

## Entry 2: 2012, Indie House opens near Steveston, Richmond; runs until late 2016

### Takeaway
**Confirmed, with refinements.** The house was acquired **around October 2012**, at Chevy Ray Johnston's suggestion. That was after TowerFall's June 2012 jam prototype, when Thorson and Johnston were still sharing a Vancouver apartment. The Georgia Straight places it "near Steveston" in Richmond and dates it "from 2012 to late 2016."

### Cited Findings
- "A little house in Richmond… a literal house near Steveston that was filled with aspiring game developers… its tenure from 2012 to late 2016." Colin Northway: "Indie House is huge in the history." Games credited to the house: *Celeste* (Maddy Thorson and Noel Berry), *Night in the Woods* and Chevy Ray Johnston's *Ikenfell*. — [Georgia Straight, Mar 27, 2024](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- After college, Thorson shared a Vancouver apartment with Johnston, whom she met through the Game Maker community. "Johnston suggested that they get a house in Vancouver to bring in others to help, which they acquired around October 2012, calling it the 'Indie House'." Thorson incorporated **Matt Makes Games Inc. in November 2012**. (She had used the "Matt Makes Games" name since April 2008.) — [Wikipedia: Maddy Thorson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson)
- "Thorson lived with a developer she met through Game Maker's community, and the two eventually moved in with Holowka in 'Indie House', a Vancouver house whose occupants are all indie developers." — [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)
- Noel Berry, on the PlayStation Blog (Dec 6, 2014): "I've been working on Skytorn over the past year alongside the developers of TowerFall Ascension and Night in the Woods in our Vancouver-based IndieHouse." — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2014/12/06/procedurally-generated-adventure-skytorn-announced-ps4/)
- Noel Berry, quoted by GamesRadar: "IndieHouse is essentially just a house of five roommates, except we are all musicians, artists, programmers and designers creating videogames." — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/digging-skytorn-towerfall-creators)
- In July 2014 Thorson was sharing the house "with four fellow game creators, all of whom contributed to the production and playtesting of TowerFall." This is Game Developer's summary of a Polygon feature, which Wikipedia lists as "What It Feels Like To Launch An Indie Hit," Chris Plante, July 2, 2014. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/looking-back-at-what-it-felt-like-to-launch-i-towerfall-ascension-i-)
- CGM (Dec 2014) calls it "Thorson's Indie House, a group-home of sorts for community friends and fellow starving artists." — [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/)
- Greg Lobanov "moved from Philadelphia to the Vancouver suburbs in 2015… and moved in with my friends in a house in Richmond." — [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)

**Residents. Documented:** Maddy Thorson, Chevy Ray Johnston, Alec Holowka, Noel Berry, Greg Lobanov (from 2015).
**Not documented as residents in what I found:** Scott Benson (*Night in the Woods* co-creator), Amora Bettany (credited TowerFall artist), Justin Smith.

### Inferences
- Five roommates in 2014 fits Thorson, Johnston, Holowka and Berry plus one more person. Bettany is a plausible fifth, but no source here confirms it.
- Suggested rewording: "c. Oct 2012: Thorson and Chevy Ray Johnston take a house near Steveston, Richmond, and Alec Holowka joins them. Noel Berry joins later and Greg Lobanov arrives in 2015. Known as 'Indie House' until late 2016."
- Several 2014 sources (the PS Blog, CGM, Polygon via Game Developer) just say "Vancouver." The Richmond and Steveston location comes from the 2024 Straight article and Lobanov.

### Gaps
- No exact address, move-out date or reason for the late-2016 end. I couldn't confirm the fifth 2014 roommate, or whether Benson, Bettany or Pedro Medeiros ever lived there. The Indie House Tumblr (indiehouse.tumblr.com) blocked automated access, so it's a lead for interviews. I couldn't open the original Polygon feature (the guessed URL 404'd).

## Entry 3: Jun 2012, TowerFall prototyped at the 48-hour Full Indie Game Jam

### Takeaway
**Confirmed.** It was the 48-hour **June 2012 Vancouver Full Indie Game Jam**. The idea came from Thorson's visit with Alec Holowka at that jam. It started as a single-player Flash prototype and became multiplayer after the jam.

### Cited Findings
- "The idea came from a visit with Alec Holowka as they worked at a game jam, the 48-hour June 2012 Vancouver Full Indie Game Jam." It began as a single-player platformer Flash game. Holowka "dropped back from the project shortly after the game jam," and demoed it at GDC 2013 when Thorson's passport had expired. MiniBoss finished the graphics. — [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)
- "Conceived by Matt Thorson during the 2012 Full Indie Jam." Full Indie had started its own annual jam in 2011. — [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/)

### Inferences
- Replace the dead CGM link and the wikipedia.org mirror with the two URLs above.

### Gaps
- I didn't find the exact jam dates (which June weekend) or venue.

## Entry 5: 2012, *Incredipede* (Northways) and *Mark of the Ninja* (Klei)

### Takeaway
**Confirmed; add dates.** *Mark of the Ninja* came out on Xbox Live Arcade on **September 7, 2012** (PC on Oct 16, 2012). *Incredipede* came out on **October 25, 2012**. Colin Northway designed it with Sarah Northway, and Thomas Shahan did the art. It was a 2013 IGF finalist. The Northways were still travelling at that point; they hadn't settled in Vancouver yet.

### Cited Findings
- *Mark of the Ninja*: Klei, published by Microsoft Studios. Announced February 28, 2012; out on XBLA September 7, 2012 and Windows October 16, 2012. — [Wikipedia: Mark of the Ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_of_the_Ninja)
- *Incredipede*: designers Colin and Sarah Northway, artist Thomas Shahan, released October 25, 2012. "Developed by Colin Northway while visiting numerous countries with his wife, Sarah." Colin found Shahan through the Wikipedia article on jumping spiders. It was a 2013 IGF finalist and was greenlit on Steam in January 2013. — [Wikipedia: Incredipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredipede)
- The Northways "spent the next five years travelling the world while making more little games, like… 2012's Incredipede." — [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- Nels Anderson was the lead designer on *Mark of the Ninja*. — [Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)

### Inferences
- *Incredipede* was made on the road, not in Vancouver. It belongs to the Northways' story, but calling it a "Vancouver release" needs that caveat.

### Gaps
- None material.

## Entry 6: Apr 2013, first Full Indie Summit at the Rio Theatre, a response to big studios and GDC Canada leaving

### Takeaway
**Confirmed, with an exact date: Saturday, April 20, 2013, at the Rio Theatre.** Brian Provinciano (VBlank, *Retro City Rampage*) came up with the idea, and he and Full Indie organized it. About 400 attendees; it sold out. The "GDC Canada left" motive is supported, but **GDC Canada ran twice, in 2009 and 2010**, before being replaced in 2011.

### Cited Findings
- "April 20 marked the first Full Indie Summit at the Rio Theatre." Brian Provinciano "came up with the idea." Speakers included Nels Anderson (Klei), Skull Theatre (Seattle), Joel Green (BioWare Montreal), Nick Yonge, Jake Kazdal (17-BIT), Adam Boyes (Sony, SCEA) and Dan Adelman (Nintendo). — [Vancouver Weekly, Apr 25, 2013](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)
- "In the absence of GDC Canada… a group of Vancouver developers have decided to throw their own conference… a collaborative effort by the Full Indie Group and VBlank's Brian Provinciano… The event takes place April 20." — [Engadget, Apr 10, 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-10-full-indie-summit-brings-vancouver-dev-industry-together.html)
- "In its inaugural year, 400 people came to the sold out event." — [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/full-indie-summit). The page is dated Dec 19, 2017 but describes the 2014 (second) summit, so that date is probably a migration artifact.
- The summit was "organized in 2013 as a response to the migration of prominent video game studios and developer conferences such as GDC Canada away from Vancouver." — [Wikipedia: Full Indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie). This cites the BetaKit piece, which I couldn't open.
- "After running for only two years in Vancouver, Game Developers Conference Canada seems to be finished. It's being replaced by the Canadian Games Conference." — [Georgia Straight, Blaine Kyllo, May 10, 2011](https://www.straight.com/node/44385)
- **Conflict:** Engadget calls 2010 "the first, and last" GDC Canada. That's wrong per the Straight piece and Game Developer's "GDC Canada 2009: The Event Round-Up" (title seen in search, not opened). Wikipedia's citation title "Full Indie Summit… on April 10" (Polygon) is the article's publication date, not the event date.

### Inferences
- Suggested text: "20 Apr 2013: first Full Indie Summit, Rio Theatre (about 400 attendees, sold out), conceived by Brian Provinciano. It fills the gap left by GDC Canada (Vancouver, 2009–2010) and the studio closures."

### Gaps
- BetaKit couldn't be opened, so the "studios leaving" framing rests on Wikipedia.

## Night in the Woods and Alec Holowka (context for Indie House)

### Takeaway
Holowka, born in Winnipeg, lived in Indie House, and Berry wrote in 2014 that *Night in the Woods* developers worked there. *Night in the Woods* (Infinite Fall: Holowka and Scott Benson, with Bethany Hockenberry) was announced on Kickstarter on **October 22, 2013** and released **February 21, 2017**. In August 2019 Holowka was accused of abuse; the team cut ties with him, and he died on August 31, 2019.

### Cited Findings
- *Night in the Woods* was announced on Kickstarter Oct 22, 2013. The $50,000 goal was hit in 26 hours, and the campaign raised more than $200,000. Companion games were *Longest Night* (Dec 2013) and *Lost Constellation* (Dec 2014). It was released Feb 21, 2017, developed by Infinite Fall and Secret Lab and published by Finji. — [Wikipedia: Night in the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods)
- Holowka (Oct 30, 1983 to Aug 31, 2019) was born in and died in Winnipeg. He co-founded Infinite Ammo, Infinite Fall and Bit Blot (*Aquaria*, IGF Grand Prize). In August 2019 Zoë Quinn accused him of physical and emotional abuse. The next day the *Night in the Woods* team cut ties with him, and Finji backed that decision. He died by suicide four days after the allegations. — [Wikipedia: Alec Holowka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Holowka)
- Holowka lived with Thorson and Johnston in Indie House. — [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)

### Inferences
- "Partly made at Indie House" (the draft's 2017 entry) is supported for Holowka's side of the work. Benson's location isn't established here.

### Gaps
- I don't know how long Holowka lived at Indie House or when he returned to Winnipeg.

## Notable 2012–2016 events missing from the draft

### Takeaway
Several well-documented Vancouver or BC milestones fall in this window and should be added: *Retro City Rampage*, Red Hook and *Darkest Dungeon*, Klei's *Invisible, Inc.* and *Don't Starve Together*, Hinterland's *The Long Dark*, the founding of Phoenix Labs, *Fantastic Contraption* VR, the *Wandersong* Kickstarter, *Celeste*'s PICO-8 origin, the dated 2014–2016 summits, and the *Night in the Woods* Kickstarter.

### Cited Findings
- **Oct 9, 2012**: Brian Provinciano (Vblank Entertainment) releases *Retro City Rampage* on PC, PS3 and Vita. — [Wikipedia: Retro City Rampage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro_City_Rampage). Vancouver Weekly calls him "one of the biggest local names." — [Vancouver Weekly](https://vancouverweekly.com/full-indie-summit/)
- **Oct 2013**: Hinterland's *The Long Dark* (Raphael van Lierop, ex-Relic) is Kickstarted, with Canada Media Fund seed money. Steam Early Access in **Sept 2014**; full release Aug 1, 2017. Note that van Lierop moved his family *out of* Vancouver (Wikipedia's text is truncated at that point). — [Wikipedia: The Long Dark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Dark)
- **Oct 22, 2013**: *Night in the Woods* Kickstarter (see above).
- **Apr 2013**: Chris Bourassa and Tyler Sigman form "British Columbia-based" Red Hook Studios. *Darkest Dungeon* Kickstarter in **Feb 2014** (more than $313,000 from about 10,000 backers); Early Access **Feb 3, 2015**; release **Jan 19, 2016**. The music is by Stuart Chatwood. — [Wikipedia: Darkest Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon). The Georgia Straight calls Red Hook "the local team behind… Darkest Dungeon." — [Georgia Straight](https://www.straight.com/tech/how-vancouver-became-a-hub-for-indie-video-games)
- **Jan 2014**: Klei's *Invisible, Inc.* enters early access; full release **May 12, 2015**. — [Wikipedia: Invisible, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible,_Inc.)
- **Dec 2014**: Klei releases *Don't Starve Together* on Steam Early Access (announced May 7, 2014). — [Wikipedia: Don't Starve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Starve)
- **Apr 2014**: Ex-Riot developers Jesse Houston, Sean Bender and Robin Mayne found Phoenix Labs in Vancouver. Its game *Dauntless* went to open beta in 2018. — [Wikipedia: Phoenix Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Labs)
- **Aug 9–10, 2014**: Full Indie Summit 2014 at the **Stanley Theatre** (2750 Granville St.). Speakers included PlayStation, Unity, Indie Fund, Vlambeer and Valve (Aaron Leiby and Chet Faliszek on VR). It grew from one day to a full weekend. — [Meetup: Full Indie Summit 2014](https://www.meetup.com/vancouver-indie-game-developers/events/195376532); [PocketGamer.biz, May 1, 2014](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/58972/full-indie-summit-returning-in-august/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/full-indie-summit)
- **Aug 2015**: Thorson and Noel Berry make the PICO-8 *Celeste* in a four-day jam, which grows into *Celeste* (Jan 25, 2018). — [Wikipedia: Maddy Thorson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddy_Thorson); [Wikipedia: Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game))
- **May 12, 2015**: *TowerFall Ascension: Dark World* expansion. — [Wikipedia: TowerFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TowerFall)
- **Oct 31, 2015**: Full Indie Summit 2015 (about 500 attendees). — [BC Living](https://bcliving.ca/?p=85325); [Game Developer blog](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/videos-full-indie-summit-2015)
- **Feb 2016**: Greg Lobanov's *Wandersong* Kickstarter raises $21,936 from 989 backers. — [Wikipedia: Wandersong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandersong)
- **Apr 5, 2016**: *Fantastic Contraption* VR (Northway Games with Radial Games; audio by Gordon McGladdery) launches bundled with the HTC Vive. — [Wikipedia: Fantastic Contraption (2016)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Contraption_(2016_video_game))
- **Oct 22, 2016**: Full Indie Summit 2016 at the **Vancouver Playhouse** (600 Hamilton St.), drawing more than 550 attendees. Speakers included Nels Anderson (*Firewatch*), Robin Baumgarten and Kert Gartner. — [Meetup: Full Indie Summit 2016](https://www.meetup.com/Vancouver-Indie-Game-Developers/events/234106569)
- **Dec 2014 context**: Full Indie "now hosts upwards of 250 developers every month." — [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/). The Full Indie Wikipedia page says it had more than 2,000 members as of 2014. — [Wikipedia: Full Indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Indie)
- **Correction for the earlier section:** CGM dates the Full Indie rebrand to **March 2011**. — [CGM Part 2](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/history-canadian-videogame-scene-part-2/)
